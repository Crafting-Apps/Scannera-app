# What the best app sites do, and what Scannera should take

Notes from Genius Scan, Signal, Proton, Things 3, 1Password, Linear, Bear,
Todoist, Raycast, Headspace and Notion, read for structure, copy and visuals.
Written against the current Scannera site so every point is actionable.

---

## The single biggest finding

**Genius Scan, the closest competitor, has no privacy section at all.**

Its page runs hero, use cases, testimonials, four features, footer. Security
appears exactly once, as a mention of its paid Genius Cloud backup, and
"Privacy" is a footer link to an external help-centre page. For an app whose
users scan passports, contracts and medical records, that is a wide open
flank.

Meanwhile the two most admired privacy products make privacy the *thesis*,
not a section:

- Signal: "Privacy isn't an optional mode, it's just the way that Signal works."
- Proton: "privacy isn't a promise, it's mathematically ensured."

Scannera is the only scanner that can honestly say the work never leaves the
phone. That should be the first thing on the page, not the fourth. The current
site buries it under a feature grid.

---

## 1. Hero: short and emotional, not descriptive

Every strong site pairs a very short headline with a plain subheadline that
says what the thing is.

| Site | Headline | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Speak Freely | 2 |
| Todoist | Clarity, finally. | 2 |
| Bear | Markdown notes you'll love | 4 |
| Headspace | Stress less | 2 |
| Raycast | Your shortcut to everything. | 4 |
| Linear | The product development system for teams and agents | 8 |
| Genius Scan | Genius Scan is a scanner in your pocket | 8 |

The two longest are the two weakest. Genius Scan's headline describes the
product category; Signal's sells a feeling.

Scannera today leads with "The Offline Document Scanner App for Android",
which is a search query, not a headline. The fix is not to abandon the
keywords but to move them: emotional H1, keyword-rich subhead, and the exact
phrases kept in `<title>`, the meta description and the section headings,
where they do the actual ranking work.

## 2. Collapse many features into three verbs

This is Notion's whole trick, and it is the one Scannera needs most.

Notion has dozens of capabilities and shows three: **Capture, Find,
Automate.** Each gets one headline, one sentence, a screenshot and an arrow
link. The full catalogue lives behind "See all features".

Scannera currently presents **eleven feature cards in one flat grid**:
scanner, batch PDF, PDF editor, QR tools, watermarks, Drive backup, OCR, App
Lock, import and organise. A visitor cannot hold eleven things in their head,
so they hold none.

Proposed grouping:

- **Capture.** Edge detection, perspective correction, shadow removal,
  filters, batch scanning, capture modes.
- **Work.** OCR in six scripts, full-text search, annotate, fill, sign,
  watermark, import, export.
- **Keep private.** On device, offline, no account, App Lock, optional Drive
  backup you control.

Three ideas, each expandable. The detail is kept, not deleted.

## 3. Give the free tier a column

Bear's pricing shows Free and PRO side by side, with the Free column honestly
listing what it does *not* include (3 themes vs 28, no iCloud sync).

Scannera's current Pro card lists six Pro features and never says what Free
gives you. A visitor cannot tell whether the app is usable without paying, so
they assume it is not. We now have the exact split from the store listing, so
a two-column table is both easy and more honest:

- Free: scan, crop, enhance, OCR, PDF export with a small Scannera watermark,
  basic drawing, QR and barcode scanning, with limits on pages per scan,
  saved documents and code scans.
- Pro: unlimited pages, documents and scans, watermark-free export, custom
  watermarks, highlights, notes, form filling, signatures, advanced QR types
  and automatic Drive backup.

Bear also frames paying as support rather than unlocking: "Help support
further development". One-time purchase makes that framing even easier here.

## 4. Social proof is everywhere except on our site

| Site | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Todoist | 347K+ five-star reviews, in the hero and again above the final CTA |
| Todoist | Three press quotes with logos, directly under the hero |
| Proton | 100M users, 4.5 stars, CNET and PCMag, independent audits |
| Linear | "over 40,000 product teams", rotating quotes with name and title |
| Things | Two Apple Design Awards, Editors' Choice, long press quotes |
| Genius Scan | Six tweet-style quotes from named people |
| **Scannera** | **nothing** |

Every single reference site has some form of it. Ours has none. Even a Play
Store rating and review count under the hero would close most of this gap.
Nothing here should be invented: use the real numbers or leave the slot out.

## 5. Structural pattern shared by all of them

Across nine sites the running order is remarkably consistent:

1. Hero: short headline, plain subhead, one primary CTA, product visual
2. Immediate credibility: logos, ratings or press, before any feature talk
3. Three to five benefit blocks, one idea each, alternating image side
4. A differentiator section that is not a feature (Signal's nonprofit status,
   Proton's Swiss jurisdiction, Todoist's 19 years, Things' awards)
5. Pricing, stated plainly
6. FAQ
7. Closing CTA that restates the hero promise
8. Dense categorised footer

Scannera has 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8. It is missing 2 and 4. Item 4 is the
interesting one: the thing that is true about you and cannot be copied. For
Scannera that is on-device processing plus a one-time price in a category
that runs on subscriptions and cloud upload.

## 6. Copy craft worth stealing

- **Rule of three with a twist.** Signal: "No ads. No trackers. No kidding."
- **Name the objection.** Proton contrasts Big Tech privacy, which protects
  you from everyone except the company, with its own.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Things: "Within the hour, you'll have everything
  off your mind."
- **Short sentences.** Things, Bear and Signal are almost entirely 5 to 15
  words. Our current FAQ answers run to 60-word paragraphs.
- **Warmth in small places.** Bear's newsletter: "No spam, ever." and "Maybe
  bear hugs, though."
- **Verb-first CTAs**, repeated at top and bottom. Todoist's "Start for free"
  appears three times.

## 7. Visual technique

- **Device mockups.** Things uses one collage of Mac, iPhone, iPad and Watch
  rather than many separate shots. We have eight real screenshots and
  currently show them in a flat gallery.
- **Editorial labelling.** Linear labels its pillars "Fig 0.1" to "Fig 0.3",
  which makes a product page feel like a specification. Cheap and distinctive.
- **One screenshot per idea.** Linear and Notion pair exactly one visual with
  each feature block. Genius Scan mixes screenshots and illustrations with no
  pattern, and it reads as inconsistent.
- **Decorative separators.** Bear uses illustrated backgrounds between
  sections to stop a long scroll feeling like a list.
- **Progressive disclosure in the nav.** Notion's mega-menu gives each feature
  an icon, a name and one line. 1Password pushes its entire depth into a
  categorised footer and shows only four cards on the page.

## 8. What not to copy

- **1Password's hero.** "Secure access for every human and AI agent" is
  enterprise positioning aimed at buyers, not users. Wrong register for us.
- **Proton's length.** Twelve sections works for a product suite. Scannera is
  one app.
- **Linear's dark theme.** It signals developer tool. Scannera's audience is
  students, small businesses and people scanning a passport.
- **Genius Scan's repetition.** Two of its use-case cards share identical body
  copy, which reads as unfinished.
- **Unsourced scale claims.** Genius Scan's "millions of documents" is
  unquantified and unverifiable, and it lands as marketing noise.

---

## Priority list for Scannera

1. Lead with privacy. It is the thesis and the competitor has ceded it.
2. Collapse eleven feature cards into three pillars.
3. Rewrite the hero: short emotional H1, keywords moved to the subhead.
4. Add real social proof under the hero, or leave the slot out.
5. Give Free its own column next to Pro.
6. Add a differentiator section: one-time price, no account, no upload.
7. Cut FAQ answers to half their length.
8. One screenshot per idea instead of a gallery.
